GoProViewer

A free macOS app to browse, preview, and copy files from your GoPro to your Mac. Plug the camera in over USB, turn it on — that's it.

Download Source on GitHub

What GoProViewer does

GoProViewer does one job: it gets footage off a GoPro camera. Plug the camera into your Mac over USB and the app shows everything on it, plays anything instantly, and copies what you choose — to a folder on your Mac, to your Google Photos library, or both.

  • Thumbnail grid grouped by capture day — chaptered videos and bursts appear as single items.
  • Preview instantly: photos zoom with a pinch, videos stream straight off the camera at full quality, and hovering a tile plays it in place.
  • Copy with resume, turbo transfer, per-day folders, original timestamps, and already-copied detection.
  • Send photos and videos to your Google Photos library — upload-only by construction, so the app can never see or touch what's already there.
  • Metadata inspector with the clip's GPS track on a map, read from the camera's own telemetry.
  • The camera is strictly read-only — the app contains no delete capability at all.

Works with any camera that speaks Open GoPro over USB — HERO10 Black and later, HERO11 Mini, HERO12, HERO13, MAX 2 — on Apple Silicon Macs running macOS 14 or newer.

Google Photos and your data

Uploading to Google Photos is optional and off until you connect a Google account. When you do, GoProViewer asks Google for exactly two permissions:

  • Add to your Google Photos library (photoslibrary.appendonly) — used only to upload the photos and videos you select. This permission cannot list, view, edit, share, or delete anything already in your library, including items the app itself uploaded.
  • Basic profile (email address) — used only to show which account is connected in the app's settings.

Uploads travel directly from your Mac to Google Photos over an encrypted connection; nothing passes through any other server, because there are none — the app has no analytics, no tracking, and no servers of its own. Your Google sign-in token is stored in the macOS Keychain and never leaves your Mac. Full details are in the Privacy Policy, and you can revoke the app's access at any time from your Google Account.
